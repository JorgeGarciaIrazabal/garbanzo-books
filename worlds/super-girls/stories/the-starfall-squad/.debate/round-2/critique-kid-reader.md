# Kid-reader critique — round 2

Round 1 was good. The book is *much* closer to a book I'd want to hear twice. The Junior-in-the-nostril gag is the funniest line in any of the six stories in this catalogue. I would request that page on the second read. The "wake up was not an option" is the moment my ears would prick up. The "eyes are still open" ending is the right ending.

But the *word cut* the round-2 author is being asked to do is a *kid-trap*. It's easy to do a word cut that makes the prose thinner without making it *snappier*. A 5-year-old will hear the difference. Here is where I, as the kid, would feel the cut going *wrong*.

**The biggest trap is page 7 (the Barnaby intro).** The round-1 prose does *two* things on this page: introduce Barnaby, and land the "I was expecting you / for approximately the last six seconds" joke. If the cut removes the *introduction* of Barnaby, the kid has to *learn* who he is from the image and from a single joke. That works *only* if the joke carries the introduction. The round-1 version has the image prompt doing the work, the description doing the work *again*, and the joke at the end. The right cut is *remove the description*. The wrong cut is *remove the joke* to make the description fit. Do not remove the joke. The joke is the only way a kid *meets* Barnaby.

**The second trap is page 11 (potato-shield).** The "I can block a grape" is the laugh line. The "grapes are very dangerous in some cultures" is the *callback* laugh. The "I want a refund" is the *kid line* — that's the line *I* would say. If the cut removes the refund, the page is *less me*. The refund is the line that turns Clara from a funny character into *my* Clara. Don't cut the refund.

**The third trap is page 22 (the sneeze).** The sneeze itself is the page. The Junior-flying-into-the-nostril beat is the line. The Catalina-flies-the-vines beat is the second line. The eye-swinging-down beat is the third line. The page should be *exactly those three beats in that order*. The "for the first time on purpose" sentence *isn't* a line a kid would say. The "like a great green net flung wide" isn't either. Both are the adult *narrating* the moment. Cut *those*. Don't cut the eye, the vines, or the nostril.

**One thing the kid-reader notices that the other two critics haven't flagged yet: the *page-turns* are better in round 1, but there's one page where the cliff is now *too soft*.** Page 19 ends on "Yes. Yes it does" — which is good, but the *next* page (page 20) opens with "Wake up" which is *backwards*. A 5-year-old hearing page 20 will think "wait, are we still in the eyes scene?" The page 19→20 transition needs the *ancient being's step* to be the cliff and the *next* page to open on the *girls' attempt to wake up failing*, not on the *girls being told to wake up*. The order of the two pages should be: page 19 = the eyes focus, the girls freeze. Page 20 = "Wake up" / "We have to DO something" / the stars go dim / the eyes step closer. The round-1 ordering is: page 19 = the eyes focus, "it sees us / yes it does". Page 20 = "Wake up" / "ON THREE" / *nothing* / the eyes get closer. The "ON THREE" is the *parent voice*. Cut it. Let the page 20 open on the *step*, not on the "Wake up" instruction.

**Also: the catchphrases.** The validator has been warning for two rounds that Clara's "I've got this", Lizzy's "Wait wait wait", Catalina's "It's okay, we'll figure it out together", Ziba's "Ooh! Ooh! I have an idea!", and Barnaby's "As the ancients say..." are *defined* in the character bibles but *never appear on a page*. A 5-year-old doesn't *know* these are catchphrases — they're *the character's voice*. The fact that they don't appear means the book isn't using the characters' voices. This is a free win. Sprinkle *one* of each into the cut. Not in a "callback" way. In a *voice* way. Ziba should *say* "Ooh! Ooh! I have an idea!" on the page where she has the idea. Clara should *say* "I've got this" before the shield cracks. Lizzy should *say* "Wait wait wait" before pointing at the pinecone. Catalina should *say* "It's okay" to Sprout in the pocket — that one is *exactly* her voice and the book *forgot* it. Barnaby should *say* "As the ancients say" before mangling a proverb. Five catchphrases, five places, the book's voice gets *five times more itself* with no plot change.

**My single biggest complaint is:** The word cut will be easy to do wrong. A 5-year-old's complaint about a word cut is not "this page is too long" but "this page is *less me*". The pages where the cut would make the book *less me* are page 7 (don't kill the joke), page 11 (don't kill the refund), page 22 (don't kill the nostril). If those three pages still land their jokes after the cut, the cut is good. If any of them don't, the cut went wrong.

**The one concrete change I'd make is:** Sprinkle the five catchphrases in the cut. *Ooh! Ooh! I have an idea!* (Ziba, page 14), *I've got this* (Clara, page 21), *Wait wait wait — I see something!* (Lizzy, page 17), *It's okay* (Catalina, page 16 to Sprout), *As the ancients say* (Barnaby, page 28 — wait, page 27). Five small adds. Each one is a line a kid would actually say. The book becomes *more me* with the cut, not less.
