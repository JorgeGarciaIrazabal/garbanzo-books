# Heckler critique — round 2

I read the round-1 revision. Two good things happened and one bad thing, and I want to talk about all three.

**Good thing #1: the climax is now a real climax.** Junior the chicken flying into the ancient being's nostril and the sneeze blowing them out is the funniest line in the book, the best *use* of the powers, and the right kind of save (stupid, accidental, by the character the book lets you underestimate). The "wake up" no longer being an option is also the right move — the book earned its fear.

**Good thing #2: the cliff is a cliff.** "~ The eyes are still open ~" as the end page is *good*. The book stops. It does not summarise. It does not moralise. It does not "and that's how they knew they were brave." Stop-on-cliff is the only correct move for an origin story and this draft did it.

**Bad thing: the word cuts landed soft.** The story is now 1586 words / 28 pages, which is *better*, but 16 of 28 pages still run 64–105 words. The minimalist critic is right that the cap is real. The author made the big cuts (page 24 → "and the eyes were still open" works) but left a lot of breathing-room prose. Specifically: pages 5, 6, 7, 9, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16, 19, 20, 22, 24, 25 — that's 14 pages where the prose is still *parent-reading-aloud* voice, not *kid-listening-in-the-dark* voice. The cuts are about word count, not about *sound*. A 5-year-old doesn't care about a 60-word cap. A 5-year-old cares that a sentence is *too long to follow in one breath*. The current prose has good jokes; it has not yet learned to *breathe* between jokes.

**Specifically: the Barnaby intro is now *too* tidy.** Page 7 reads like a children's book that knows it's a children's book. The "I was HERE! Expecting!" gag is good, but the *setup* of it (the dignified puff-up, the owl face, the upside-down star patch) is being *described* to the reader, not shown. The image prompt is doing the showing; the text is restating it. Cut one. Trust the picture.

**The Junior gag landed perfectly. Use it harder, in fewer places.** Junior saving the day is the right beat. Junior the *mascot* — Junior on the ear, Junior on the note, Junior in the title — is starting to feel like the book *noticed* its own joke and is now *managing* it. If you sprinkle Junior on every page, he's a brand. If he's only on the pages where the joke actually lands, he's a *character*. He's on pages 14, 15, 16, 22, 23, 25, 27 — that's seven pages. Cut it to four. The pages where the joke lands: 14 (the chaos), 22 (the nostril), 24 (a quiet joke on Clara's cracked-star page), 27 (the feather drifting past the eyes). Those four. Cut the rest.

**One real concern: the prose got *softer* in the cuts, not tighter.** Read page 22 aloud. The sneeze scene works — but the sentences around it are doing the work of the *old* prose. "It was the biggest sneeze in the history of the Dreaming" is parent-voice. "A breath. Cold. So cold the candy-trees went white" is *much* better — that's the voice you want. The first sentence wants to be the second sentence. So the round-2 question is not "is it over the cap" but "is every sentence the *tightest* version of itself?" The kid-reader will catch the ones that aren't.

**My single biggest complaint is:** The cuts so far have been *quantitative* — word count came down, the cap is closer, but the prose in the still-over-cap pages is still doing parent-voice. A 70-word page is not the same thing as a 60-word page if the 60 words inside are still soft.

**The one concrete change I'd make is:** Take pages 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 22 — the six pages where the prose is doing setup *and* payoff in the same breath — and *split* the setup from the payoff. The image carries the setup. The text carries the punchline. Trust the page to do half the work. Page 7 should be: the landing, "MY DIGNITY!", the introduction, the *one* line of upside-down-star description, and Barnaby's "I have been expecting you" / "for approximately the last six seconds" — that's the page. Cut the dignified-puff-up description, cut "stitched by someone who had never held a needle before", cut the round-up. Same beats, half the words, twice the snap.
