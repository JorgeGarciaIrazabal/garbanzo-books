# Minimalist critique — round 2

The round-1 revision took the *macro* cuts — the four meet-cute pages became one, the climax was rebuilt, the manuscript went from 2442 → 1586 words. That's the right direction. But the *micro* cuts weren't done, and now the manuscript is sitting on the word-per-page cap with a hard validator failure.

Concretely: 16 of 28 pages still run 64–105 words. The cap is 60. The validator calls this a `FAIL`. The publisher button will refuse to flip the book to "published" with this on. So this isn't a craft preference; it's a *publishing* requirement. The round-2 author needs to do the mechanical word-cut work.

**My instructions to the round-2 author, by page:**

- **Page 1 (montage opener, 68w)** — the Gerald-the-fern line is doing the work of a running joke, but it's also the third reference to Gerald (after the original Clara/Lizzy/Catalina/Ziba intros that got cut). Pick one place for the joke. Cut the second and third.
- **Page 2 (the four bad dreams, 64w)** — *"Clara dreamed she was trying to protect someone and couldn't move. Lizzy dreamed the answers were on a wall, in a language she couldn't read. Catalina dreamed her plants were wilting and no water helped. Ziba dreamed everything was gray. No colors. No sparkles. Just gray."* — five sentences, four dreams, two of them have a *commentary* sentence after them. The "no colors, no sparkles, just gray" sentence is the punchline. Cut the rest of the dreams down to one beat each. Target: 50 words.
- **Page 5 (oak tree / reckless thing, 76w)** — the "is it contagious aliens" / "you can't just SAY things" dialogue is doing the work of *two* jokes. Keep the contagious aliens joke, cut the "you can't just say things" thread, cut the "reality is flexible right now" beat. Target: 50 words.
- **Page 6 (tunnel drop, 73w)** — the "I DID NOT APPROVE THIS / YOU APPROVED IT FIVE SECONDS AGO / I HAVE CHANGED MY MIND / YOU CAN'T JUST SAY THINGS" exchange is the joke. The "oh wait" button at the end is the punchline. Cut every line *between* the "five seconds ago" and the "oh wait" — they are repeats. Target: 50 words.
- **Page 7 (Barnaby intro, 79w)** — the description of Barnaby (owl face, rabbit ears, round body, star patch) is *setup*. The dialogue (his "expecting you", the "six seconds" beat) is *payoff*. The image is doing the setup. Cut the prose setup. Keep the dialogue. Target: 50 words.
- **Page 9 (Cloud of Destiny, 76w)** — "the famous Broken Candy Cane Tree, which I just invented" is a one-line punchline. The "Lizzy wrote in her notebook, 'this is our guide, we are doomed'" is a *second* punchline on the same page. Pick one. Cut the other. Target: 50 words.
- **Page 11 (potato-shield, 73w)** — the joke lands on "I can block a grape". The "grapes are very dangerous, in some cultures" beat is the second joke. The "I want a refund" line is the third. Pick two of three. The refund is the funniest. Target: 55 words.
- **Page 12 (lantern pinecone, 77w)** — the "scientific breakthrough" / "we could see the pinecone with our eyes" / "BUT NOW WE KNOW" is *one* joke told in three lines. Pick the strongest single line, cut the other two. Target: 55 words.
- **Page 13 (Catalina vines, 82w)** — the joke lands on "modern art, very cutting edge". The "I am experiencing a minor setback" line is the setup. The image is doing the setup. Cut the "minor setback" line. Target: 55 words.
- **Page 15 (sawing Catalina free, 81w)** — the "I have a squeaky weapon / intimidation through tiny sounds / brilliant / that's not a thing / it is NOW, I invented it" exchange is *two* jokes told in five lines. Pick the squeaky weapon beat *or* the intimidation-through-tiny-sounds beat, not both. Target: 50 words.
- **Page 16 (regroup, 74w)** — the page does *three* jobs: Clara upgrades the shield, Lizzy's lantern changes focus, Catalina's vines start behaving. Split this into *two* spreads, not one. The Lizzy focus-shift is the better cliffhanger — keep the Lizzy beat on this page, move the Clara upgrade to the previous page's end (it's a one-sentence add). Target: 50 words.
- **Page 19 (Dreaming goes silent, 71w)** — the "frozen mid-bubble" image beat is the best line. The four stars' "blazing like tiny suns" beat is the *parent voice*. Cut one. The "it sees us" / "yes it does" exchange is the cliff — keep it. Target: 55 words.
- **Page 20 (first wake-up fails, 80w)** — the page does *two* jobs: the girls try to wake up, *and* the ancient being takes one step. The being's step is the better cliffhanger. Cut the wake-up attempt, end on the step. Target: 55 words.
- **Page 22 (the sneeze, 105w)** — the longest page in the book, and the most important one. Cut the exposition. The page should be: the eye swings down → a cold breath → Catalina flings the vines → Junior flies into the nostril. That's it. No "for the first time on purpose." No "like a great green net flung wide." No "Big as houses." The image does all that. Target: 70 words. (It's the climax; it gets 10 extra.)
- **Page 24 (Clara wakes, 71w)** — the cracked-scar on the star is the detail. The dream-feather is the detail. The "it wasn't a dream / it had never been a dream" pair is *parent voice*. Pick one line. Target: 50 words.
- **Page 25 (oak tree / Barnaby, 88w)** — the "I have been expecting you" gag is round-1's best line. The "Junior is a mystery I cannot solve" callback is the new line. The cocoa detail and the tire-swing detail are both *atmosphere*. The page is doing four jobs. Cut the cocoa (it's been done in round 1; the kids know). Cut the tire swing. Keep the mystery line. Target: 55 words.

That's the cut list. After it's done, the manuscript should sit at ~1300 words, every page ≤ 60 words, and every cut *should have made the surviving prose punchier* — not thinner.

**My single biggest complaint is:** The cuts in round 1 happened at the *page* level (deleted pages 1–4 in their entirety). The cuts in round 2 need to happen at the *sentence* level — inside the surviving pages — and the test is not just "fewer words" but "is every sentence the tightest version of itself?" Right now, several surviving pages have *one* good sentence and three sentences of soft parent-voice around it.

**The one concrete change I'd make is:** Every page in the over-cap list above gets the cut pattern *"trust the image; trust the punchline; cut the setup"*. The result is not a *thinner* book — it's a *sharper* one.
