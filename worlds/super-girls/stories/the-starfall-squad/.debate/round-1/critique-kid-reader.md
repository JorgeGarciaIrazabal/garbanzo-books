# Kid-reader critique — round 1

I've watched this book as if I were the 5-year-old at bedtime who's heard the first half three times and is *done* with the second half. Here's where I'd get up, leave, or ask for a different book.

**The biggest problem is pages 1–4.** A 5-year-old doesn't need a *tour* of four characters they've never met. They need a *reason to care about one of them right now*. Page 7 is where Clara discovers the glowing star and the book finally *does* something. Pages 1–4 are dead air. A 5-year-old will ask "when does it start?" three times before page 5. Then they'll ask "is it starting yet?" twice more before page 7. That's the moment the parent thinks "let's skip to the good part" and never comes back to the intros.

**The second problem is page 14 — "The important part."** Barnaby announces that the girls have powers. *"The Starlight Spirits chose you because each of you has a power. A gift. Inside you."* A 5-year-old *will not* sit still for "a power, a gift, inside you." They'll say "WHEN DOES SHE GET THE POWER." The whole page is *one line* of useful information: *"You have powers. Discover them."* That's the page.

**The third problem is page 24 — "ON THREE. THINK ABOUT YOUR BED."** This is the part where the book is supposed to be the most exciting. Ancient being. Green eyes. Mountain-sized. The girls are in danger. **And then they... wake up.** No fight. No almost-fail. No "the green eyes followed them home." Just... wake up. A 5-year-old will say "wait, that's IT?" This is the most disappointing page in the book, and it's the *climax*. I have watched children close books in disgust at less.

**The fourth problem is the *page-turns*.** A good picture book ends almost every spread on a question or a cliff. This book ends *a lot* of spreads on a *period*. Examples:

- Page 4 ends on "She narrates her own life out loud. 'Ziba opened the door. Ziba tripped on nothing. Ziba is fine.'" — period. No cliff.
- Page 5 ends on "There was only one weird thing." — *what* thing? Cliff's a setup, not a delivery.
- Page 11 ends on "One ear flopped. '...for approximately the last six seconds.'" — that's a punchline, fine, but the next page is a long *describing* page instead of a beat that builds.
- Page 20 ends on "'There's something out there,' she whispered." — good cliff, but the *next* page (21) is a travel montage, not a payoff.
- Page 22 ends on "It is BIG. And old. And —" — strong cliff, followed by 200 words of Barnaby explaining, not by the eyes opening.
- Page 24 ends on "The world turned gold. Everyone thought about waking up. Very hard." — that's a *resolution* ending, not a cliff.

**The *single best page-turn* in the book is page 7** — *"She put a band-aid over it. The star glowed straight THROUGH the band-aid."* That's a kid-tested great turn. There should be *ten more like that*.

**The single best joke in the book is "I can block a grape?"** A 5-year-old will laugh there. The next best is "SCIENTIFIC BREAKTHROUGH! THIS PINECONE EXISTS!" A 5-year-old will laugh there too. The third is the chicken pecking Barnaby on the beak. These are the three places a kid will request the second read. The rest of the book is *almost* at this level but the rest of the book also has 28 pages to get there in. Less pages, more laugh-moments, more kid-altitude page-turns.

**One more thing — a 5-year-old's favourite character will be Barnaby.** That's almost certainly true. The book should *know* this. Right now Barnaby has good lines but he doesn't *do* anything a kid is rooting for. He needs one moment where the kid yells "GO BARNABY!" — a moment where he saves the day by *accident* and is *immediately* confused by the credit, and the girls are like "yes, we saw, you're a fraud, also thank you." The book is *one Barnaby-moment* away from being a kid's favourite.

**My single biggest complaint is:** Page 24 is the climax and it is a *letdown*. The book sets up a mountain-sized ancient being with green eyes and then resolves the threat with "think about your bed." A 5-year-old will feel cheated. The book promised them a *chase*, *almost got caught*, *barely escaped*, and instead gave them "and then they woke up."

**The one concrete change I'd make is:** Rewrite page 24 so the girls' *first attempt* to wake up *fails*. The stars go dim. The eyes get closer. They have to *do something* with their newly-discovered powers to escape — Clara's shield cracks but holds, Catalina's vines pull them up, Ziba's last chicken leads them out — and they escape *mid-chaos*, the green eyes still on them, the chicken now leading the way. The DOOR-sized shield can arrive on the next page, but it should arrive *spent* — cracked, dimming, just enough. The cliff is "it knows us now." Not "we're ready for tomorrow."
