# Minimalist critique — round 1

The manuscript is **2442 words** against a 1500-word soft cap for 5–7. **22 of 28 pages exceed the 60-word-per-page cap.** That's not "could be tighter" — that's structurally bloated. The book has the material for a 14–16 spread picture book; it's been written as a 28-spread chapter book. Every page is doing two things, and most of them are doing the *setup* of one thing and the *payoff* of the previous.

**The biggest problem is the four meet-cute pages (1–4).** Pages 1, 2, 3, 4 each introduce one girl with a list of her traits. They are *adjacent*, almost the same structure, and the information density is near zero. By the end of page 4 a kid knows: Clara has a plan, Lizzy has questions, Catalina has plants, Ziba has a marker. This is a character sheet, not a story. **These four pages can be one spread**, or replaced by a montage page (the existing page 5 already does this — and does it better than the four intros do). Pick one approach: either the four intros *or* the montage. Not both.

**The second problem is the *because_of_that* list in the spine doing 5 separate beats across pages 47–56, each one already expanded to 2–3 pages of prose.** The book keeps the cause-effect structure *but* pads each beat with explanation. A 5–7 year old is hearing the same beat three times: once in the spine, once in the prose, once in the moral. The spine and the prose should be the *same* thing, not two parallel tracks.

**Specific page cuts I'm proposing (in priority order):**

- **Pages 1–4 (four meet-cutes)** → one spread. Or drop them entirely; the montage page (5) already establishes them as a group.
- **Page 6 (the four bad dreams)** — keep, but the dreams are described twice (once in `text`, once in the image `prompt`). Cut the image prompt's four-panel description; the text can carry it. Saves 30+ words on the prompt.
- **Page 14 (the powers intro speech)** — Barnaby explaining "you have powers, a gift, inside you" and Ziba asking "like... in my stomach?" is the cute page. The information ("you have powers, discover them") can be one line. Cut the rest.
- **Page 19 (sorting out the chicken mess)** — this is a *transition* page that exists because the previous pages had so much chaos. If the chaos is tighter, the transition is one beat, not a full page.
- **Page 22 ("two eyes... green as bottle glass... ah, said Barnaby... that")** — the dialogue is doing the work of the prose. *"Ah. That. I was... definitely going to mention that. ... BARNABY. ... The Dreaming has... residents. Old ones. Very old. Very big. I maybe forgot."* That's the same beat said three ways. Pick one.
- **Page 28 (Barnaby's "the Dreaming is bigger than I said" speech)** — this is exposition retrofitted to a scene that already happened. The exposition should be *during* the first tour, not after the cliffhanger. Move the line, lose the page.

**The biggest word-per-page offender is page 6 (the four dreams) at 130+ words.** That's a 60-word page turned into a short story. Split it into a montage image + a one-line caption: *"Every night. The same bad dream. A shadow in the corner. Watching."* Trust the illustration to do the work.

**The book has 4 interaction pages and the validator says it wants ≥5.** That's not a quantity problem; it's a *pacing* problem. The interactions land on pages 8, 12, 18, 26. Page 26 is the *jigsaw* of Ziba's note, which is the worst possible moment to interrupt a cliffhanger. Move the jigsaw to page 5 (the friendship montage) and add a different interaction — a `tap-on-art` for "tap each girl's power to see it in action" — to a page where it serves the story, not pauses it.

**My single biggest complaint is:** Pages 1–4 (the four girl-intro pages) plus the corresponding section in the montage (page 5) are the same beat said three times. They are also the lowest-energy pages in the book. They are also where the prose most sounds like a *parent* introducing kids at a playdate, not a story. The story doesn't start until page 7, when Clara discovers the glowing star.

**The one concrete change I'd make is:** Delete pages 1–4 in their entirety. Keep page 5 as the *only* character introduction. Trust the montage. Move the title-page energy to a single illustration of the four girls under the oak tree at recess (which is the actual story location) rather than four separate bedroom portraits. This single cut removes ~250 words, gets the manuscript under 2200, and gets the story moving by page 2.
