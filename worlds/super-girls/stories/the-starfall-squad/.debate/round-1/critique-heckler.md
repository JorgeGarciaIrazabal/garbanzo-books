# Heckler critique — round 1

This draft has *good bones* but it's reading like a book that's afraid of itself. The first half is a parade of "This is X. She has a Y. She does Z." — meet-cute pages that don't earn their place. The middle is where the mischief actually lives (the potato-shield, the chicken attack, Barnaby's broken candy cane) and it's genuinely funny. The back half is where the book gets nervous and starts moralising, soft-pedalling, and pulling its punches.

**The biggest problem is the ending.** The "mountain-sized ancient being with green eyes" is the best thing in the book and the book is *terrified of it*. The girls don't fight it. They don't even *see* it properly. They just go home. Wake up. Find a feather. Pass a note. End on a *resolved* note about how brave they're going to be next time. That's a pilot-episode cop-out dressed up as a cliffhanger — and a kid will smell it. The thing with the green eyes is the *promise* of the book. The book breaks that promise. Then the "potato → bowl → DOOR" shield upgrade at the end is a *level-up animation* in a game — it has no cost, no cost, no consequence.

**The second problem is Barnaby's cover-up.** The reveal that he "forgot to mention" the ancient dream-monsters is the right joke, but then the book lets him off the hook *completely* — the girls don't punish him, he doesn't get a comeuppance, he's just sitting in the tire swing waiting with cocoa. A kid wants to see the moment where Clara makes him apologise, or he has to earn the cocoa back, or something. Right now the funniest character in the book is also the one with no consequences.

**The third problem is the prose doing the parent's work.** Lines like *"Modern art! Very cutting edge!"* and *"Intimidation through tiny sounds! Brilliant!"* are the author *narrating* the joke. Barnaby saying them is fine; the structure around them telling us they're jokes is the cute page. And the character-intro pages (1–4) are almost *Curious George* register — "This is Clara. She has a plan. She ALWAYS has a plan." That's the parent voice, not the story voice.

**The chicken gag is the engine and the book should commit harder.** Junior the chicken refusing to leave Barnaby's ear is the single best running joke in the book. Use it. The "elevenses" page where it makes Junior invisible, the note-passing page where it's somehow on the note, the final heroic page where it sits on top of the gold shield. The chicken should be the *unofficial fifth member* of the squad, and the book should know it.

**Quotes to flag:**

- Page 24, "ON THREE. THINK ABOUT YOUR BED. Your pillow. Your blanket. THREE —" — the book is afraid of the ancient being. It needs to *almost* fail, almost get caught, escape with one star going out.
- Page 26, "Something had happened. Something real. And terrifying. And AMAZING." — that's the parent doing the throat-clearing. Cut it.
- Page 29, "Clara stood up. Feet planted. Arms crossed. Captain stance. ... it was the size of a DOOR. ... ~ The adventure is just beginning ~" — this is a *tween* ending, not a picture-book ending. It explains the joke. The DOOR-sized shield is the joke; stop right after the line, not after explaining it.
- Page 11 — the meet-cute with Barnaby is *too* meet-cute. He doesn't have a *plan*. He has "I was HERE! Expecting!" but he wasn't expecting anything. Make the first meeting the moment Barnaby is already mid-disaster, not mid-pose.

**My single biggest complaint is:** The ending of the book — pages 22–29 — turns the mountain-sized ancient dream-being into a non-event, lets Barnaby off the hook, and ends on a *video-game level-up* instead of a real cliff. The promise of the book is broken on the last third.

**The one concrete change I'd make is:** Pages 22–29 need a complete rewrite. The girls don't just "think about waking up" — they try, *fail* (the green eyes follow them through the dream), have to *do something* to escape (Catalina's vines shield them, Clara's shield cracks but holds, Ziba's last chicken confuses the ancient being long enough — *use* the powers they just discovered), and they escape *mid-disaster*, not mid-pose. Barnaby has to do *one real thing* to help, and the chapter ends on a moment of "we escaped but it *saw* us" — not "we're ready now." The DOOR-sized shield is great, but it should arrive on the second-to-last page and *crack* on the last page, so the cliffhanger is real.
