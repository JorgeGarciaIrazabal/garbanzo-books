# The Magical Forest — Style Guide

## Art Direction

A soft watercolor storybook world at fairy size — everything is **big, soft, and glowing**. Ferns are like tall roofs; a puddle is a big shiny lake; a dropped berry is a rock to climb. The mood is cozy and full of wonder — like a warm hug in a magic wood.

**Medium**: Soft watercolor with thin ink lines and a soft pencil feel. Colors bleed into each other at the edges. Lines have a little wobble — this world feels hand-made and warm, never stiff or flat.

**Lighting**: Warm sunbeams poking through leaves — soft golden light, no hard shadows, no cold blue light.

**Point of view**: Low, like a kid looking up. The camera sits close to the ground so every mushroom looks tall and every dew drop is a glass ball. Fairies are shown at their own size in their big world.

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Show the world from fairy height — nuts are big, grass blades bend over like arches
- Use soft, blurry watercolor edges — nothing should feel sharp or flat
- Add tiny cozy bits: a nut-shell door, a puff of sweet smoke from a hut top, a dew-drop lamp
- Keep faces soft and warm — wonder, joy, sly little grins, mild pouts
- Let one or two fireflies glow in scenes near Pond Place

**Don't:**
- No hard lines or flat shapes — no flat-digital look
- No big people, no big-people things — the world is pure fairy
- No scary faces, sharp teeth, or truly scary scenes (a bit spooky is okay; scary is not)
- No cold blue light — warmth is a must
- No words or signs in the art itself

## Inspirations

Beatrix Potter's soft animal worlds, Cicely Mary Barker's Flower Fairies, the lush woods of Studio Ghibli, and the warm glow of Trina Schart Hyman — all mixed with cozy fun and daily fairy life.
